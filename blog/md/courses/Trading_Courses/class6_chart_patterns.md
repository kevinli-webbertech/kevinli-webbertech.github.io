# Class 6 - Chart Patterns

## Goal

Recognize recurring chart patterns that signal continuation or reversal.

## Topics

- Head and shoulders (and inverse head and shoulders)
- Triangles: ascending, descending, symmetrical
- Flags and pennants
- Double tops and double bottoms

## Definitions

- **Head and shoulders**: A bearish reversal pattern with three peaks, the middle one (head) higher than the two surrounding peaks (shoulders).
- **Inverse head and shoulders**: A bullish reversal pattern with three troughs, the middle one (head) lower than the two surrounding troughs (shoulders).
- **Ascending triangle**: A continuation pattern with a flat upper resistance line and a rising lower support line, usually resolving bullishly.
- **Descending triangle**: A continuation pattern with a flat lower support line and a falling upper resistance line, usually resolving bearishly.
- **Symmetrical triangle**: A pattern where both the upper resistance and lower support lines converge toward each other, with the breakout direction determining the bias.
- **Flag**: A short, rectangular continuation pattern that slopes against the prevailing trend before the trend resumes.
- **Pennant**: A short continuation pattern similar to a small symmetrical triangle, forming after a sharp price move.
- **Double top**: A bearish reversal pattern where price reaches a similar high twice with a moderate pullback in between.
- **Double bottom**: A bullish reversal pattern where price reaches a similar low twice with a moderate bounce in between.

## Key Takeaways

- Reversal patterns (head and shoulders, double top/bottom) signal a change in trend.
- Continuation patterns (flags, pennants, triangles) signal a pause before the trend resumes.
- Pattern confirmation requires a breakout with volume, not just the visual shape.
