# Class 1 - Market Basics

## Goal

Understand how financial markets work before touching a chart or placing a trade.

## Topics

- What is a stock, an exchange, and a ticker symbol
- Market participants: retail traders, institutions, market makers
- Order types: market order, limit order, stop order
- Bid/ask spread and how trades get filled
- Market hours, pre-market, and after-hours trading
- Brokerage accounts: cash vs. margin accounts

## Definitions

- **Stock**: A share of ownership in a company, giving the holder a claim on part of its assets and earnings.
- **Exchange**: A regulated marketplace (e.g., NYSE, Nasdaq) where stocks and other securities are bought and sold.
- **Ticker symbol**: A short, unique set of letters (e.g., AAPL) used to identify a publicly traded company's stock on an exchange.
- **Retail trader**: An individual who buys and sells securities for their own personal account, rather than for an institution.
- **Institution**: A large organization (mutual fund, pension fund, hedge fund, bank) that trades securities on behalf of clients or itself, usually in large volume.
- **Market maker**: A firm or individual that continuously quotes both buy and sell prices for a security, providing liquidity to the market.
- **Market order**: An order to buy or sell immediately at the best available current price.
- **Limit order**: An order to buy or sell at a specified price or better; it may not fill if the market never reaches that price.
- **Stop order**: An order that becomes a market order once a specified "stop" price is reached, typically used to limit losses or protect gains.
- **Bid/ask spread**: The difference between the highest price a buyer is willing to pay (bid) and the lowest price a seller is willing to accept (ask).
- **Market hours**: The official trading session during which an exchange is open (e.g., 9:30 AM - 4:00 PM ET for US markets).
- **Pre-market trading**: Trading that occurs before the official market open, usually with lower liquidity and wider spreads.
- **After-hours trading**: Trading that occurs after the official market close, also typically with lower liquidity and wider spreads.
- **Cash account**: A brokerage account where trades must be fully paid for with available cash; no borrowing is allowed.
- **Margin account**: A brokerage account that allows an investor to borrow money from the broker to purchase securities, using existing holdings as collateral.

## Key Takeaways

- Every trade has a buyer and a seller matched through the exchange's order book.
- Limit orders give you price control; market orders give you speed.
- Understanding order types is the foundation for every strategy covered later in this course.
