# Class 5 - Technical Indicators II

## Goal

Expand the indicator toolkit with MACD, Bollinger Bands, RSI divergence, and volume analysis.

## Topics

- Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD): signal line and histogram
- Bollinger Bands: volatility and mean reversion
- Relative Strength Index (RSI): revisiting overbought/oversold and spotting divergence
- Volume-based indicators (On-Balance Volume)
- Reading indicator divergence vs. price

## Definitions

- **MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)**: A momentum indicator showing the relationship between two EMAs of price, used to spot changes in trend strength and direction.
- **Signal line**: An EMA of the MACD line itself; crossovers between the MACD line and signal line are used to generate buy/sell signals.
- **MACD histogram**: A bar chart showing the difference between the MACD line and the signal line, visualizing momentum shifts.
- **Bollinger Bands**: A volatility indicator consisting of a moving average with upper and lower bands plotted at a set number of standard deviations away from it.
- **Mean reversion**: The theory that price tends to move back toward its average after moving too far away from it.
- **Relative Strength Index (RSI)**: A 0-100 momentum oscillator introduced in Class 4; here we focus on using it to spot divergence rather than just overbought/oversold levels.
- **Bullish RSI divergence**: Price makes a lower low while RSI makes a higher low, suggesting selling momentum is fading and a bounce may be near.
- **Bearish RSI divergence**: Price makes a higher high while RSI makes a lower high, suggesting buying momentum is fading and a pullback may be near.
- **On-Balance Volume (OBV)**: A cumulative volume-based indicator that adds volume on up days and subtracts volume on down days to gauge buying/selling pressure.
- **Divergence**: A situation where price and an indicator move in opposite directions, often signaling weakening momentum and a potential trend change.

## Key Takeaways

- MACD crossovers can signal momentum shifts.
- Price touching the outer Bollinger Band does not automatically mean reversal; check volatility context.
- RSI divergence (price vs. RSI direction) often gives an earlier warning than overbought/oversold levels alone.
- Divergence between price and an indicator often precedes a trend change.

## Practice

Identify one MACD crossover and one Bollinger Band squeeze on a chart of your choice, and describe what happened to price afterward. Then find one example of RSI divergence (bullish or bearish) on the same or a different chart, and note whether price followed through on the divergence signal.
