# Class 4 - Technical Indicators I

## Goal

Introduce the first set of technical indicators: moving averages and RSI.

## Topics

- Simple Moving Average (SMA) vs. Exponential Moving Average (EMA)
- Golden cross and death cross
- Relative Strength Index (RSI): overbought and oversold levels
- Combining moving averages with RSI for confirmation

## Definitions

- **Simple Moving Average (SMA)**: The average closing price over a set number of periods, giving equal weight to each period.
- **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**: A moving average that gives more weight to recent prices, making it more responsive to new price changes than the SMA.
- **Golden cross**: A bullish signal that occurs when a shorter-term moving average (e.g., 50-day) crosses above a longer-term moving average (e.g., 200-day).
- **Death cross**: A bearish signal that occurs when a shorter-term moving average crosses below a longer-term moving average.
- **Relative Strength Index (RSI)**: A momentum oscillator, scaled from 0-100, that measures the speed and size of recent price changes.
- **Overbought**: A condition, often RSI above 70, suggesting a security may have risen too far too fast and could be due for a pullback.
- **Oversold**: A condition, often RSI below 30, suggesting a security may have fallen too far too fast and could be due for a bounce.

## Key Takeaways

- Moving averages smooth out price noise to reveal the underlying trend.
- RSI above 70 suggests overbought; below 30 suggests oversold (not a guaranteed reversal signal).
- Indicators work best combined with price action, not in isolation.

Add a 50-day and 200-day SMA to a chart. Note any recent golden cross or death cross events.
